Tellurium atoms (produced via fission) typically occupy oxygen vacancies inside ZrO₂ with a tiny defect volume of 2.74 Å³. However, through radioactive beta minus decays, Te decays to I, Xe, and finally Cs. Over time, the lattice site is forced to accept atoms with significantly larger sizes. This transmutation increases defect relaxation volumes significantly, accumulating massive internal lattice stresses and leading to crack propagation inside the claddings.

Core Problem explored

Zirconium alloys are highly suited as nuclear cladding materials due to unique properties. However, during operations, pellet-cladding interactions (PCI), especially chemical interactions involving corrosive iodine fission products, limit cladding lifespan and raise structural concerns. This thesis employs DFT models to determine precisely how iodine enters, prefers defect sites, and transmutates within the oxide layers formed on claddings.

Key Discoveries

  • Iodine defects preferentially adopt +1 or -1 oxidation states in monoclinic and tetragonal zirconia structures.
  • At high oxygen partial pressures ($p_{O_{2}}$), iodine defects in tetragonal Zirconia fall significantly due to competition.
  • Steady-state reactor calculations confirm significant Average Iodine populations (approx 1.3g per assembly).

Zirconia (ZrO₂) Crystal Polymorphs Comparison

Zirconia undergoes structural transformations with temperature, radically affecting defect volumes and stress states.

Monoclinic (m-ZrO₂)

Stable < 1443 K
Space Group: $P2_1/c$
Coordination: Zr: 7-fold, O: 3 & 4-fold
Iodine behavior: Adopt +1/ -1 oxidation state. Small changes with $pO_{2}$

Tetragonal (t-ZrO₂)

Stable 1443 - 2643 K
Space Group: $P4_2/nmc$
Coordination: Zr: 8-fold, O: 4-fold
Iodine behavior: Significant drop under oxidizing conditions. Highly stable.

Cubic (c-ZrO₂)

Stable > 2643 K
Space Group: $Fm\bar{3}m$
Coordination: Zr: 8-fold, O: 4-fold
Iodine behavior: Extremely sensitive to defect cluster symmetry breaks.
